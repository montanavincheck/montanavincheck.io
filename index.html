Which States Are Cracking Down on Montana Plates

You've likely seen it. An automobile parked on Beverly Hills streets, a Ford F-250 in suburban Chicago, a brand new RV in a Salt Lake City driveway. All of the automobiles wear Montana license plates. There's no accident; the "Montana Loophole" created the environment for that. By the time 2026 rolls around, states that have lost millions of dollars due to the loophole will stop turning their heads. Below explains how it works, which states are cracking down on it, and what to check if you're purchasing a car with a Montana tag.

How the Montana Loophole Works

Montana does not charge any type of state sales tax and charges low registration fees. Additionally, the state allows non-residents to create a Limited Liability Company (LLC). Therefore, an auto owner creates a Montana LLC and has the LLC purchase the vehicle. Then the LLC has the vehicle registered in the company's Montana address. For as little as $1,000, registered agents will take care of everything. The owner of the vehicle will never set foot in Montana. On paper, the vehicle is owned by a Montana company. However, the vehicle exists in a garage located approximately 1000 miles from Montana.

There's a catch, however. Creating the LLC itself doesn't put people into trouble. When the car is being driven and maintained mostly in the home state of the owner of the vehicle, that state can still collect sales or use tax, registration fees, and emissions compliance.

California: the most aggressive state so far

California has been the loudest regarding this issue. Its Tax Agency (CTDFA), along with its Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) announced the results of a three-year investigation in early March 2026:

  • Approximately 500 auto dealers connected to over 2,500 sales using Montana registrations
  • 14 individuals charged by the State Attorney General in a 57-count indictment, including conspiracy to commit tax evasion, submitting false sales tax returns, money laundering, and perjury
  • Approximately $4 million collected in unpaid taxes and fees
  • Investigators discovered some auto dealers submitted fake shipping documentation so that customers could leave the dealership with Montana-registered vehicles

Additionally, California utilizes Automated License Plate Readers (ALPRs) to track the presence of vehicles displaying Montana plates traveling throughout California. They then compare those findings to registration and tax records.

Reporting in the Fall stated that California took further action. A new law, SB 1406, became effective at the end of September 2026. According to reporting coverage, it focuses on residents utilizing out-of-state LLCs for vehicle registration and can make LLC members responsible for paying taxes owed. Since it is relatively new, how it will be enforced remains unclear.

Utah: Letters followed by payments

Utah addressed Montana-registered vehicles with a notification campaign. Utah first sent notifications to 1,608 registered vehicle owners. Utah reported collecting $1.6 million in taxes along with $32,500 in penalties from 558 residents. Reporting in September stated Utah estimated as many as 80,000 vehicles could exist within this scheme; therefore, this process is far from complete.

Illinois, Indiana, Georgia & Missouri

Multiple states have moved through new legislation:

  • Illinois enacted a law allowing Illinois to "look through" the LLC and hold an individual resident accountable for unpaid vehicle-related taxes. This directly opposes the primary reason for establishing an LLC.
  • Indiana enacted legislation intended to prevent improper out-of-state vehicle registrations, including Montana-based LLCs.
  • Georgia has established enforcement mechanisms pursuant to legislation enacted in 2025.
  • Missouri introduced legislation in 2026 to prohibit residents from registering vehicles outside of Missouri to avoid Missouri taxes and imposed possible penalties such as fines and loss of driver's license. Please verify Missouri's current legislative status because pending bills are subject to change.

Additional states pursuing this loophole

Iowa, Massachusetts, and Illinois have aggressively pursued Montana-registered vehicles for years. Tennessee received widespread media attention when it indicted YouTuber Whistling Diesel for felony tax evasion for posting an internet video showing him destroy a Montana-plated Ferrari. Coverage also identifies Wyoming as one of the states increasing enforcement efforts, although I did not find strong evidence supporting Wyoming's involvement in this area.

To summarize: California, Utah, Illinois, Indiana, Georgia, and Tennessee represent clear examples, while Missouri and additional states appear to be enacting similar legislation.

How states detect it

States do not need to see any flashy activity to identify potential issues. States employ multiple detection methods including:

  • License plate reader systems mounted on police cruisers, toll plazas, and parking garages tracking how frequently Montana plates appear within proximity of each other
  • Records provided by auto dealerships indicating local purchases of vehicles having been sold under Montana registration
  • Automated cross-referencing across DMV records and tax records and property records
  • Reports and tips received during traffic stops resulting in inquiries regarding who owns and maintains operation of the vehicle

Ultimately, when a Montana plate is consistently seen driving within your neighborhood, that represents a trend, and trends reveal themselves via data collection.

Implications for Used Car Buyers

This affects consumers most as a buyer rather than simply as taxpayers. A used car with Montana plates may possess secondary consequences for you as well:

  • Registration/title difficulties - Depending upon whether or not the state determines the registration was improperly completed, you may incur back taxes or penalties or may experience delays or denial in registering your car again.
  • Ownership Clarification - The LLC is considered the official owner of the vehicle on paper. Be certain that the seller has actual power to sell the vehicle prior to sale. Also request LLC documentation.
  • Seller evading questions about past usage - If the seller avoids answering your questions pertaining to where they believe the vehicle has traveled, regard that as a red flag.
  • Gaps in Emissions/Inspections - Vehicles lacking proper registration/emissions inspections/registration in your home state may fail registration upon attempting to register your car.
  • Registration costs - Prepare for payment of your state's sales/use tax and fees when registering your car in your name. Before registering your car, contact your DMV for information regarding their exact requirements.

Complete basic procedures: Verify VIN number, Research Vehicle History Report, Run lien reports, Obtain Independent Mechanic Inspection.

If You Already Have a Montana LLC Registration

I am neither a lawyer nor an accountant; therefore, all applicable laws vary by state. If you reside in one of the above-mentioned states and utilize a Montana-registered vehicle there, it would be wise to consult with both a Tax Attorney/Certified Public Accountant. Some states provide voluntary disclosure programs which allow delinquent parties to file before being detected by authorities and potentially receive reduced penalties. Consult with experts in order to determine if you qualify for such relief.

If you truly reside in Montana or store your vehicle outside your home state, you should remain organized, retain records, and seek guidance from professionals relative to your unique circumstances.
